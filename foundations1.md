I think that's the right approach. Rather than generating a huge roadmap in one go, we'll treat this like writing a book. Each phase will be carefully analyzed, cross-checked, and reviewed before moving on. That will produce a much higher-quality result.

## How we'll build it

We'll create **four complete roadmaps**:

1. **Foundations**
2. **Low-Level Design (LLD)**
3. **High-Level Design (HLD)**
4. **AI Systems Design**

Each roadmap will be divided into phases.

For **every phase**, we'll include:

* Learning objectives
* Prerequisites
* Topics (from beginner to advanced)
* Subtopics (nothing important omitted)
* Common mistakes
* Hands-on exercises
* Mini projects
* Recommended open-source repositories to study
* Books, papers, blogs, and videos
* Completion checklist
* Interview topics (where applicable)

This will be designed as a **production engineering curriculum**, not just an interview guide.

## My plan for quality

I don't want to rely only on memory. For each phase, I'll:

* Compare topics against typical Computer Science curricula.
* Cross-reference senior software engineering expectations.
* Include topics expected in Staff/Principal engineering roles.
* Add AI-specific concepts where they naturally fit.
* Verify that prerequisite relationships are correct so you don't encounter concepts before you're ready for them.

The goal is to produce something that is comprehensive, logically ordered, and useful both for learning and as a long-term reference.

I recommend we spend the time to do this thoroughly—even if it takes many iterations—rather than rushing through it. The end result should be a curriculum you can realistically follow over the next 1–2 years, with no significant gaps.
